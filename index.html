Abstract

This paper presents a systematic survey of tools that use large language models (LLMs) to support literature reviews. This emerging generation of tools is transforming the field by enabling the generation of outputs that resemble human-written reviews. We analyse 35 systems, including 21 academic and 14 commercial tools. We systematically compare these systems using a framework of 22 features covering system design, workflow capabilities, usability, and evaluation. We provide an in-depth analysis of the techniques, models, and resources currently employed, highlighting prevailing approaches and emerging trends. We also identify key limitations of the current landscape and outline a research agenda structured around eight challenges. These include moving beyond the summarisation of individual sources towards critical synthesis, establishing shared evaluation practices, improving transparency, explainability, and verifiability, and developing human-in-the-loop approaches that preserve researcher control and accountability. Overall, this survey aims to provide researchers and practitioners with a comprehensive understanding of this rapidly evolving landscape and a foundation for developing the next generation of LLM-based literature review systems.
